# Daily Influencer Scan

Use this before each daily writing run when current Naver Blog or Tistory style learning is requested. The goal is to learn current editorial patterns, not to copy posts.

## Required Scan Sources

Use Naver first, then Tistory.

### Naver Blog

Visit current examples from:

- Naver Blog search results for the day's target keywords.
- Naver integrated search blog results and popular-content blocks when shown.
- Naver Blog posts from creators ranking repeatedly for practical information, shopping guides, event summaries, seasonal issues, or household/product comparison topics.

Prefer posts that are:

- Recent enough for the topic.
- Clearly aligned with the same reader intent.
- Rich in comments, sympathy, saves, or visible engagement when available.
- Written as practical experience, comparison, checklist, or reader-help content.

### Tistory

Visit current examples from:

- Tistory search or category/discovery pages when available.
- Tistory blogs surfaced in search results for the day's target keywords.
- Search queries scoped to `site:tistory.com` for the same topic, season, product group, or issue.
- Individual Tistory blogs where recent/popular post modules are visible.

Prefer posts that are:

- Structured for search with headings, tables, source notes, or summaries.
- Recent enough for the topic.
- Ranking or recurring for similar informational intent.
- Useful for Tistory adaptation, not Naver voice imitation.

## Daily Minimum

For each daily run, observe:

- 5 to 7 Naver Blog examples.
- 3 to 5 Tistory examples.
- At least 2 examples from the exact topic family of today's posts.
- At least 2 examples from adjacent high-performing categories for layout inspiration.

If browsing is unavailable, use `influencer-style-learning.md`, `style-memory.md`, and `sample-bank.md`, and clearly state that the current daily scan was skipped.

## What To Capture

Record only abstract observations:

- Title hook pattern.
- Opening question or situation framing.
- First-screen density: text/image/summary balance.
- Paragraph length and blank-line rhythm.
- Heading style and section order.
- Image placement: thumbnail, first image, checklist, comparison image, product image.
- Emoji/emoticon frequency and placement.
- Table, bullet, FAQ, checklist, or comparison block usage.
- CTA or comment question.
- Trust devices: checked date, source note, watch-out, who should skip.
- First visual type and whether it matches the opening situation.
- Visual-role sequence across the post.
- Caption quality and the presence of visual proof.
- One truthful editorial action that makes the post feel checked rather than generated.

Do not save:

- Full sentences from another creator.
- Unique jokes, catchphrases, personal stories, or identity markers.
- Image files or image URLs for reuse.
- Private account data, browser session data, or login-dependent information.

## Pattern Extraction

Convert each observation into this form:

- Source type: Naver Blog or Tistory.
- Topic family:
- Observed pattern:
- Why it may work:
- Reusable version:
- Risk to avoid:

Score each example from 0 to 2 on:

- Search-intent fit.
- First-screen clarity.
- Visual-role diversity.
- Original utility such as a decision table, map, calculator, checklist, or scenario.
- Trust and source handling.

Do not learn from an example scoring below 7 out of 10 even if its title is attractive. Visible engagement is a clue, not proof of quality.

## Update Rule

Update `influencer-scan-log.md` after each scan.

Update `style-memory.md` only when:

- The same pattern appears in at least 3 observed posts, or
- The pattern appears in both Naver and Tistory with the same reader benefit, or
- The user's real performance data later supports it.

Do not update `style-memory.md` from one attractive post alone.

## Safety Gate

Before using a learned pattern in a draft, confirm:

- The final wording is original.
- The structure is common enough to be reusable.
- The post adds new reader value with current facts, comparison, checklist, or cautions.
- The draft would not look like a near-clone if the original creator saw it.
- The pattern improves a reader decision rather than merely making the post look busy.
