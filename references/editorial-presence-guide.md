# Editorial Presence Guide

Use this after research and before the final Korean rewrite. The goal is an original post with the presence of a careful human editor, not an imitation of a specific influencer.

## What Creates The Feeling

Build the post from six signals:

1. Reader scene: begin with a recognizable moment, decision, or frustration.
2. Editorial point of view: state what matters first and what can wait.
3. Visual proof: show a relevant scene, source-backed evidence, and a useful decision aid.
4. Section rhythm: alternate answer, explanation, example, caution, and visual breaks.
5. Trust: name checked dates, limits, uncertainty, and who should skip.
6. Next action: end with one realistic step, not a generic request for engagement.

## Truthful First Person

First person is allowed only for work actually performed during the run:

- "공식 안내를 다시 확인해보니"
- "조건을 금액대로 나눠보면"
- "결제 화면에서 먼저 볼 부분은"
- "여러 안내를 비교해보면 공통으로 남는 조건은"

Do not claim purchases, visits, tests, conversations, or personal results that the user did not provide.
Read `evidence-and-experience-policy.md` and state the package's experience basis before using any first-person experience.

## First-Screen Pattern

Within the opening 5 to 8 mobile lines:

1. Name the reader's immediate situation.
2. Give the quick answer or decision order.
3. Explain why the detail matters today.
4. Add one limit or exception.

Place a scene-first thumbnail immediately after this opening. Do not use a generic text card as the first visual when the topic has a recognizable place, object, room, product category, or action.

For an experience-backed post, replace the generated scene with the strongest user-owned evidence photo and name the observation date or changed condition near the first screen.

## Section Construction

Use two or three of these moves in each major section, not all of them mechanically:

- Answer: the one-sentence conclusion.
- Reason: why the conclusion follows.
- Scenario: budget, family type, room size, date, or usage condition.
- Watch-out: the exception that changes the answer.
- Decision: what the reader should do next.

Include at least two grounded reader scenarios in a long post. Keep them hypothetical and clearly framed, such as "아이와 2박을 잡는 경우라면" or "판매금액이 경계선에 걸렸다면".

## Headings And Bridges

Prefer headings that carry a decision:

- "숙소보다 객실 표시를 먼저 보세요"
- "7만 원 경계에서 할인이 달라집니다"
- "취소 전에는 재발급 조건부터"

Avoid a long run of encyclopedia headings such as "개요", "특징", "장점", "주의사항".

Use bridge sentences sparingly. Do not repeat "확인하세요", "중요합니다", or "정리하면" as a fixed ending.

## Visual Captions

Write one short caption below every body visual. A useful caption explains why the image is there:

- "할인액은 숙박 일수와 판매금액을 함께 봐야 합니다."
- "공식 안내에서 적용 지역과 유효시간을 먼저 확인했습니다."

Do not use captions such as "관련 이미지" or repeat the heading verbatim.

## Anti-Template Pass

Before delivery, reject the draft when:

- Every section has the same paragraph count.
- Three or more visuals use the same card layout.
- The first image is a text-only summary card despite a visual topic.
- The post could fit any keyword after replacing a few nouns.
- Cautions appear only at the end.
- The closing says only "도움이 되셨길 바랍니다" or asks for generic comments.

## Presence Check

Score each item 0 or 1. Revise below 5 out of 6:

- The first screen names a real reader situation.
- The post gives a clear editorial priority.
- At least two concrete scenarios are present.
- Visuals serve different roles.
- Trust limits appear near the relevant claim.
- The closing gives a useful next action.
