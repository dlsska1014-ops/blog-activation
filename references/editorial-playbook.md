# Editorial Playbook

## Daily Research

Browse current sources before writing about anything time-sensitive: prices, product availability, event dates, search trends, platform rules, benefit eligibility, legal or policy details, and news.

Collect:

- 3 to 5 current reader questions.
- 3 to 5 factual source notes with checked dates.
- 5 to 10 title patterns from high-performing Naver Blog or Tistory posts in the same topic area.
- Repeated layout signals: paragraph length, spacing, table use, image rhythm, comparison sections, and conclusion prompts.

## Topic Selection

Prefer topics with practical reader intent:

- "What is the benefit and who can get it?"
- "What should I buy first?"
- "What is the best value combination?"
- "What changed today?"
- "What are the risks or hidden conditions?"
- "How do I apply, buy, compare, or avoid mistakes?"

For event-shopping posts, cover:

- Event summary, dates, target stores or products, limits, exclusions, and checked date.
- Best-value buying scenarios.
- Recommended purchase list by budget.
- Mistakes to avoid.
- Short FAQ.

## Draft Structure

Use this structure unless the topic suggests a better one:

1. Title options: include one search-friendly, one curiosity-led, and one benefit-led title.
2. Opening: start with the reader's concrete question or situation.
3. Quick answer: summarize the key point in 3 to 5 lines.
4. Main sections: use short headings, short paragraphs, lists, and tables where comparison helps.
5. Image notes: mark where screenshots, product photos, charts, or comparison images should be inserted.
6. FAQ: answer 3 to 5 likely questions.
7. Closing: give a practical next step, not a generic ending.
8. Tags: include Korean search tags and platform tags.

## Natural Korean Style

Aim for helpful human editorial writing, not "AI detection evasion."

- Mix sentence lengths.
- Use plain Korean and concrete nouns.
- Add light conversational bridges such as "여기서 중요한 건", "생각보다 헷갈리는 부분은", and "정리하면".
- Use emoticons or emoji sparingly when they fit the user's blog tone.
- Avoid repeating the same sentence endings.
- Avoid fake certainty, fake experience, and exaggerated claims.
- Avoid over-polished corporate phrasing.

## Draft Quality Check

Before delivery, check:

- Does the first screen answer why the reader should keep reading?
- Are dates, prices, rankings, and benefit conditions current?
- Is the post original rather than a close paraphrase?
- Are tables used only where they improve scanning?
- Are image placement notes specific?
- Does the post include a draft-save checklist?
